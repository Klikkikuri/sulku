# Ensemble Voting & Generalist Fallback Architecture

## Overview

`sulku` employs an ensemble of fastText classifiers to detect AI-generated text. Each model in the primary ensemble is a **specialist**, fine-tuned to recognize stylistic markers from a specific AI generator family (e.g., GPT-style, Claude-style, Llama-style).

This document details the architectural rationale, statistical formulas, design trade-offs, and fallback strategies used to combine prediction scores without suffering from specialist dilution or selection bias.

---

## The Specialist Dilution Problem

When text generated by a specific model (e.g. GPT-4) is evaluated by a multi-model ensemble:
1. **Specialist Match**: The GPT-specialist model yields a high AI probability score (e.g., $0.85$).
2. **Out-of-Distribution Specialists**: Non-matching specialists (e.g., Claude or Llama detectors) evaluate the text near their human baselines (e.g., $0.15$ to $0.25$).

### Why Naive Approaches Fail

- **Simple Averaging**: Taking the mean of all models ($\frac{0.85 + 0.15 + 0.20}{3} = 0.40$) penalizes the correct detection and results in a false negative.
- **Unweighted Majority Voting**: Two mismatched specialists outputting low AI scores will outvote a single matching specialist.

---

## Tier 1: Per-Model Z-Scores & Stouffer Combination

To allow specialist models to flag AI text without being dragged down by out-of-distribution models, we normalize scores into Z-scores and combine them using a modified Stouffer's method.

### 1. Offline Baseline Calibration
Each model $i$ is calibrated against a dataset of verified human text to estimate its baseline performance:
- $\mu_i$: Mean prediction score on human text.
- $\sigma_i$: Standard deviation of prediction scores on human text.

These constants are stored in `MODEL_CALIBRATION`. Uncalibrated models fall back to `DEFAULT_HUMAN_MEAN = 0.5` and `DEFAULT_HUMAN_STD = 0.10`.

### 2. Z-Score Normalization
Given a raw prediction score $s_i \in [0, 1]$ from model $i$:
$$z_i = \frac{s_i - \mu_i}{\sigma_i}$$

### 3. Clipped Z-Scores (No Selection Bias)
When a specialist model evaluates text outside its domain, its low prediction score results in a negative Z-score ($z_i < 0$). This reading indicates "this text does not match my generator", which is uninformative rather than strong evidence of human authorship.

To remove this uninformative noise without introducing selection bias (which occurs if denominator $k$ varies based on active models), we **clip negative Z-scores to zero** while keeping a **fixed denominator**:
$$z_i^+ = \max(0.0, z_i)$$

### 4. Fixed-Denominator Stouffer Combination
We combine the clipped Z-scores over all $k$ loaded models using Stouffer's formula with fixed denominator $\sqrt{k}$:
$$Z_{\text{combined}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k z_i^+}{\sqrt{k}}$$

- **Decision Rule**: The document is classified as AI if $Z_{\text{combined}} \ge Z_{\text{threshold}}$ (where $DEFAULT\_Z\_THRESHOLD = 1.0$).

---

## Tier 2: Generalist Fallback Tier (Design)

### Motivation
If $Z_{\text{combined}} < Z_{\text{threshold}}$, no specialist model exhibited a strong positive signal. This happens when:
1. The text is genuinely human-written.
2. The text was generated by a novel or unlisted AI generator not represented in the specialist ensemble.

Relying on majority voting among specialists in this case is unreliable because specialists have already failed to agree.

### Design Mechanism
When Tier 1 is inconclusive, the system triggers a **Tier 2 Generalist Fallback**:
1. Query a **Generalist Classifier** trained on pooled AI text from all known generators vs. human text.
2. Calculate the generalist score $S_{\text{gen}}$.
3. If $S_{\text{gen}} \ge \text{GENERALIST\_THRESHOLD}$, flag as AI (`is_ai = True`) with metadata `fallback_triggered = True`.
4. Otherwise, classify as Human.

---

## Summary of Known Issues & Trade-offs

| Feature | Advantage | Trade-off / Known Issue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Z-Score Normalization** | Accounts for per-model variance and baseline shifts. | Requires periodic offline calibration on human text. |
| **Clipped Z-Scores** | Eliminates out-of-distribution noise from non-matching specialists. | Ignores strong human-evidence signals if a model is trained as a human detector. |
| **Fixed Denominator ($\sqrt{k}$)** | Avoids selection bias across varying model counts. | A larger ensemble requires slightly higher positive Z-scores to cross the threshold. |
| **Generalist Fallback** | Catches novel/unlisted AI generators. | Requires training and loading an additional generalist model. |
